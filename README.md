# 🎱 Royal Billiard 8 — Masters League

An 8-ball pool game for phones and computers. The game is `index.html`; the online part (real opponents, friends, invite bonuses, global leaderboard, real-money purchases) is the small Node.js server in `server/`.

## Features
- **Always landscape and full screen:** on the first tap the game goes full screen and locks to landscape. On phones that can't lock (iPhone Safari), the game rotates itself. When installed as an app (PWA), it opens full screen in landscape. While the page is turned (a phone held upright, e.g. inside Telegram with the screen's rotation locked) the phone's own keyboard would stay upright at the side of the turned page, so every text box there gets the game's own keyboard (Latin / Cyrillic / digits / emoji) on the page's bottom edge; its ⌨ key hands the box to the phone's keyboard.
- **Languages:** O'zbekcha, English, Русский, Türkçe, Español, Português, Français, Deutsch, Bahasa Indonesia, العربية, हिन्दी, 한국어, 日本語, 中文, ไทย (Thai), Tiếng Việt (Vietnamese) (🌐 button). Picked automatically from the player's device language, or from their country (found from the device time zone) when the device is set to English. Everything on screen follows the chosen language — menus, settings, popups, toasts, tutorial, in-game banners, names on the tables and in the leaderboards' default player names — so no word is left in Uzbek. To add or change a text: put a line `uz key‖en|ru|tr|es|pt|ar|id|hi|fr|de|ko|ja|zh|th|vi` into `tools/xt.txt` (15 values; 13 if en/ru are already in DICT; 2 = only th|vi) and run `python3 tools/build_i18n.py`. Thai and Vietnamese (all 1807 strings, boot texts, `CC_LANG` TH->th / VN->vi, picked automatically in Thailand / Vietnam) are machine-quality drafts: a native speaker should read them once; the chat phrases are translated too, but the quizzes (server) fall back to English in these two languages. Test: `node tools/test_lang_thvi.js`. Server announcements (`/admin/news`, `POST /api/news/post`) may carry per-language texts in an optional `i18n` object (`{"en": {"title", "text", "btn"}, "de": {…}}`); a language without its own copy falls back to English.
- **Country:** detected automatically (time zone, then browser region) and shown as a flag on the leaderboards; players can change it in the profile.
- **Online 1 on 1:** real players are matched per city. Both devices get the same rack, shots and aim are streamed live, and chat works between players. If nobody is found within 15 s, a computer opponent (marked 🤖) plays instead.
- **Friends:** each player has a unique ID code. A friend code or a name in *Suggested friends* sends a **friend request** — the other player gets a pop-up with **Accept / Decline** (declined: nothing is added and nothing is said; accepted: both appear in each other's friend list, and the asker is told). **Share** opens Telegram's chat picker inside the Mini App (the link `t.me/<bot>/<app>?startapp=f_CODE` makes a new player a friend at once, with the invite bonus; anybody else sends a request). Friends can **chat** (pop-up with quick phrases, unread badges, swear filter, 80 messages / 60 days kept per pair) and start a table together with one tap; one gift a day to each friend, gift requests, and who is online (active in the last 2½ minutes). **Bot messages:** a player who signed in through the Mini App and allowed it (Telegram's pop-up, asked once when the Friends screen opens; or `/start` in the bot chat) gets a message from the bot — in their game language, with a button that opens the right screen — when they are away from the game and someone sends a friend request, accepts theirs, writes to them, or invites them to a table (friends only). At most 15 a day, one per sender per few minutes, nothing while the game is open; switch off in Settings → *Notifications in Telegram* or with `/stop`; a blocked bot is skipped.
- **Invite bonus:** the inviter gets 🪙 20 000 and the invited friend gets 🪙 8 000 (coins only: 💎 are never given away). It works with an invite link (`?ref=CODE`) or by entering the code in the first 7 days.
- **Weekly leaderboards (like 8 Ball Pool):** My League / Friends / Country / World tabs, ranked by coins won this week (Monday–Sunday UTC). Prizes for the world top 3 each week: 🪙 1 500 000 / 750 000 / 400 000 (country top 3: 400 000 / 200 000 / 100 000).
- **Leagues:** Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum → Diamond → Master → Grandmaster. Everyone starts in Bronze; each week the top 20% of a league move up and the bottom 20% move down.
- **322 cues in 5 tabs** (every generated cue has its own colour and pattern pair; a cue button next to the spin ball lets you switch or buy cues during a match): Standard (coins or 💎), Victory (collect 4 pieces by winning in a city or higher), Surprise (legendary cues, pieces from Surprise Boxes), Country (a flag cue for each of 199 countries) and Owned. Collection power grows with every cue.
- **Avatars and frames:** 100 avatars (20 free, the rest for coins or 💎) and 9 avatar frames.
- **Expensive economy:** 18 cities like 8 Ball Pool — London, Sydney, Lisbon, Tokyo, Las Vegas, Jakarta, Toronto, Cairo, Dubai, Shanghai, Paris, Rome, Bangkok, Seoul, Mumbai, Berlin, Istanbul, Osaka (100 000 000 entry / 200 000 000 prize).
- **Tournaments:** Asia Cup (32 players), South America Cup (32), Europe Cup (48), World Cup (60) — knockout brackets with byes, 5–6 rounds.
- **Clubs** (unlocked at level 6): create or join a club (up to 50 members), club chat, members ranked by weekly winnings; members of the week's top 3 clubs get 🪙 20 000 / 10 000 / 5 000.
- **Season pass ("King's Gambit"):** a monthly season with 30 tiers, a free and a premium track (700 💎), daily missions, exclusive cues and frame.
- **Cloud save:** the whole progress is saved on the server (Postgres) a few seconds after every change and when the page is hidden; a newer save on the server is restored on start. Settings → *Get key* gives a recovery key; on a new phone *Restore* with the ID + key signs in to the same account.
- **Settings:** sound, vibration, cue sensitivity, tap to aim, aiming wheel, guideline in offline games, power bar side, cheering, friend requests, online status, friend-online notifications. Coin cues go up to **5 billion**; premium cues go up to **1 500 cash** (💵) or the same value in 💎. Cash is hard to earn (1–5 at a time).
- **Currencies:** 🪙 coins (free), 💵 cash (earned in play or bought, a real-money-grade currency) and 💎 diamonds (the premium money that is never free), with the game's own artwork. The top bar shows all three; the 💵 chip (with the balance) opens the cash packs.

In the game each picture is laid as 240-unit tiles centred on the table, every second tile mirrored (no joins show, marble veins meet like book-matched slabs), an oval shade towards the walls; until its sheet has loaded only the dark base shows (a few ms). The Rose table keeps its hall picture. `node tools/test_halls.js` checks that every table has its own picture, that all sheets decode, the numbers are valid and no picture is used twice.
- **Hall pictures** (`ROOM_SRC` / `ROOM_FOR` in `index.html`, `tools/add_room_plate.py`): a table can stand in a real picture of a hall instead of the drawn one. The picture is scenery only (16:9, the middle of it empty floor): the game draws the table, the bars and the buttons itself, the picture is scaled to cover the whole screen and centred on the table. `/usr/bin/python3 tools/add_room_plate.py <picture> <name> --for e_ros,... [--erase x0,y0,x1,y1 --marble]` stores it as a WebP in `ROOM_SRC` and lists the tables in `ROOM_FOR` (`--erase` paints a table / buttons that were baked into a sample out of the picture). Only the Rose table (`e_ros`) uses one now (`rose`, made from the owner's sample); every other table has its own drawn hall.

## Run it yourself
```bash
cd server
npm install
npm start            # http://localhost:8080
```
Open `http://localhost:8080` and the game connects to the server automatically. Opening `index.html` as a plain file also works, but online features are then off.

## Put it on the internet for free (Render + Neon)
Player data is stored in Postgres when `DATABASE_URL` is set; without it the server uses a JSON file (local use).
1. **Database:** create a free project at neon.tech and copy the connection string (`postgresql://…?sslmode=require`). The server creates its tables itself.
2. **Server:** on render.com choose **New → Blueprint** and pick this repo. `render.yaml` sets everything up (free plan, root `server`).
3. Fill in `DATABASE_URL` (the Neon string) and `APP_URL` (`https://billiard8.onrender.com`). Leave the Stripe keys empty for now.
4. The log should say `storage postgres`. Open the URL and play.

The free Render plan sleeps after ~15 minutes without visitors; the first visitor then waits about 30 s. Data is safe in Neon either way. A paid instance removes the sleep.

## Fast loading: the light build (`web/`)
`index.html` is one 40 MB file (every picture and sound is inside it). Opened from Render it would be downloaded again and again, so the repo also holds the **light build** in `web/`:
a 4 MB page plus the pictures (`img/<hash>.webp`) and sounds (`a/<hash>.wav|mp4`) as files. `server/server.js` serves `web/` itself (same origin, so the API works as before):
* the page is downloaded once (about 2 MB gzipped), the pictures only when a screen shows them; their names are hashes, so they are sent with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` and the phone keeps them - an update downloads only the changed page and the new pictures;
* `sw.js` (service worker, where the browser allows it) keeps the pictures too (cache first) and never copies a huge page; a new cache name drops the old 40 MB copy;
* **after every change of `index.html` run `SAME_ORIGIN=1 INLINE_UNDER=6000 NO_ZIP=1 python3 tools/build_web.py web` and commit `web/`.** The file `web/.src` holds the hash of the `index.html` it was made from; if they differ (you forgot) the server ignores `web/` and serves the old single-file page, `/api/health` shows `"web": false`;
* `node tools/test_web_serve.js` checks it all: light page, immutable pictures, 304, Range for the video, the game boots and a match starts, a second visit downloads nothing, a stale build is ignored.

### Real-money payments (Paddle — preferred; works for sellers where Stripe is unavailable)
Paddle is the merchant of record (takes the card payment, handles tax, pays you out). The server builds each transaction itself from the SKU price, so no products need to be created in the Paddle catalog.
1. Paddle → Developer tools → Authentication: create an **API key** (permissions: Transactions, Customers, Addresses, Prices, Products read+write; Notifications read) and a **client-side token**.
2. Paddle → Developer tools → Notifications: add a destination `https://your-domain/api/paddle/webhook` for the event `transaction.completed` and copy its **secret key**.
3. Set on the server: `PADDLE_API_KEY`, `PADDLE_CLIENT_TOKEN`, `PADDLE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (live) — or the same names ending in `_SANDBOX` for testing (sandbox is used when no live key is set, or when `PADDLE_ENV=sandbox`).
4. Checkout opens from `/pay?_ptxn=…` (served by the game server; the domain must be approved in Paddle → Checkout settings for live payments). If Paddle is configured it is used instead of Stripe.

### Real-money payments (Stripe, alternative)
1. Create a Stripe account and get your **Secret key**.
2. Add a webhook endpoint `https://your-domain/api/stripe/webhook` for the event `checkout.session.completed` and copy its **signing secret**.
3. Set `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` and `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` on the server.

Prices live in `SKUS` in `server/server.js` (the server is the source of truth). Purchases are credited to the player's account by the webhook, and the game picks them up automatically.

> ⚠️ **App stores:** if you publish to Google Play or the App Store, their rules require their own in-app billing for digital goods (Google Play Billing / Apple IAP), not Stripe. Stripe is fine for the web version.

### Telegram payments (inside the Mini App)
- **Stars** (default, always on when `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` is set): `POST /api/stars/invoice`; `STARS_PER_USD` (default 55) sets the price.
- **Card provider through Telegram Payments** (GlobalPay, ...; so'm, so offered to Uzbekistan players only - `isUzPlayer()` in the page, `p2pUz` on the server; abroad nobody sees so'm, the foreign card shows dollars): off until `TG_PAY_TOKEN` is set in Render. It is the provider token BotFather shows under *Payments* - **use the provider's TEST token first** (no real money). Then the buy button in Telegram asks "Stars or card" and `POST /api/tgpay/invoice` makes the invoice. `TG_PAY_CURRENCY` (default `UZS`) and `TG_PAY_PER_USD` (default 12700; the card price is rounded to whole 100 so'm) set the price. Telegram's own rules say digital goods in bots / Mini Apps are sold with Stars: check them before putting a live token in.

### Card-to-card payments (a transfer to the shop's own card, no payment provider)
In Telegram the buy button also offers "Card transfer" and / or "Via ATM" when these are set in Render (both OFF otherwise):
- `P2P_CARD` - the card number players pay to. Several cards (up to 4, e.g. Humo + Uzcard) go in one value separated by commas: `9860 1606 4515 2044|Aziz A., 8600 1111 2222 3333` (`number|name` gives a card its own holder name; the kind - Humo / Uzcard / Visa / Mastercard - is shown from the first digits). `P2P_HOLDER` - the name shown under cards without their own (optional). **Who sees which card:** `number|name|uz`, `|world` or `|all`; by default Humo / Uzcard are shown to players in Uzbekistan (or with no country set) and Visa / Mastercard to players of every other country. A foreign card works with the receipt flow only (the price is shown in dollars, the admin confirms): money from abroad never arrives as one exact so'm amount, so it cannot be matched from an SMS. A card marked `all` is shown to everyone. Either card can be paid: the order is recognised by its amount, so the phone must forward the SMS of every card. `TG_PAY_PER_USD` (default 12700) - so'm per 1 USD of the card price.
- **Card transfer (automatic)** - needs `P2P_NOTIFY_KEY` (a long random secret). The player gets a unique, not round amount (price + 1..99 so'm) and must send exactly that. The shop phone forwards every bank SMS to `POST https://<server>/api/p2p/notify?key=<P2P_NOTIFY_KEY>` (JSON `{"text": "<the SMS>"}`, the SMS as a plain body, or whatever shape the forwarder app sends: the text is found in any string field, also nested ones like `{"payload": {"message": ...}}`; Google Play does not allow apps that forward SMS to a URL, so these apps come from GitHub / F-Droid: Incoming SMS to URL Forwarder (bogkonstantin), TextBee, httpSMS, SMS Gateway for Android). An order is paid when its exact amount appears in an SMS. The order lasts 30 minutes.
- **Via ATM (receipt)** - needs `ADMIN_TG_ID` (your Telegram user id: send `/id` to the bot) and that you pressed Start in the bot. The player sends a photo of the receipt, the bot sends it to you with Confirm / Reject buttons. The order lasts 3 days.
- By hand: list the open orders with `curl $SERVER/api/p2p/pending -H "x-admin-key: $ADMIN_KEY"`, then `curl -X POST $SERVER/api/p2p/confirm -H "x-admin-key: $ADMIN_KEY" -d '{"id":"<order id>","ok":true}'`.
- Limits: 3 open orders per player; every payment is counted once (a repeated SMS or button press pays nothing more). Refunds are manual. Check the bank's rules about receiving shop payments on a card, and Telegram's rules for digital goods, before using it.

## Watching a live match (spectators)
A watcher plays the two players' shots on his own screen (the same deterministic physics) and is put right by the shooter's `state` (all ball positions, the turn, the groups) after every shot. What makes it hold together, and what broke on Y8 once (tested by `node tools/test_spec_sync.js`, MODE=normal|slow):
- **Message numbers.** The server numbers `shot` / `state` messages **per seat** (each seat counts 1, 2, 3 ...). A player gets messages from one seat only, so the page's duplicate filter (`rxN`) used one counter per match; a watcher gets BOTH seats and half of the shots / positions were thrown away as "duplicates": the balls jumped, the turn went wrong, the cue stood still. The server now sends spectators no `n` at all (this cures old pages / apps too, once the server is deployed) and the page keeps one counter per sender seat.
- **No shot is dropped** (`specShot()`): a shot that arrives while the previous one still rolls on a slow screen shows the shooter's final positions of the previous shot at once and goes on from them (or waits in `G.specQ` when they are not here yet). The `shot` message carries the shooter's shot number `no`, so the physics dice (`simSeed`) are the same for a watcher who came in late.
- **The turn.** Whoever sends `aim` holds the turn (a watcher whose own simulation got the turn wrong is put right by the first `aim`); a watcher never ends the match from his own simulation (`specEnd` from the server does), sends no `aim` and does not count balls into his own statistics.
- **The turn clock.** The ring round the avatar of the player who has the turn goes down for a watcher too (`drawPanel`).

## Channel posts (the auto-poster)

The game's public channel is `https://t.me/Royalbilliard8` (button in Settings and under the bot's welcome message; `TELEGRAM_CHANNEL_LINK` overrides the address). The server keeps it alive with **three posts a day for as long as it runs** (about 100 days of posts without a repeat, then the lists start again). It is off until you switch it on: make the bot an **administrator of the channel with the right to post messages** (channel > Administrators > Add), set `CHANNEL_AUTOPOST=1` on the server and redeploy. Keep the server awake (UptimeRobot on `/api/health` every 5 minutes): a sleeping free instance cannot post. A server that slept through a slot catches up later the same day, one post per 45 minutes, and what is left after 22:45 is dropped.

**When.** Three slots in Tashkent time: about **10:00, 14:00 and 20:30** (a small jitter of up to 15 minutes, fixed per date, so a restart does not move it). At least 45 minutes between two posts, at most slots + 2 a day. Only the first post of a day makes a sound; the others go out silently (`disable_notification`).

**What.** The kind of a post depends on its slot:

| slot | kind | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| morning | `feat` | a feature of the game (voice room, open lobbies, gifts, stickers, cues, tables, frames, avatars, ranking, cups, pass...) with a banner |
| afternoon | `quiz` | a Telegram **quiz poll** (billiards rules, history and stars (Mosconi, Reyes, O'Sullivan, films, famous people who loved the game), geography of the 30 city tables, facts of the game) with the right answer and a short explanation |
| evening | rotates `city` / `shop` / `tip` / `poll` / `story` | one of the 30 city tables with its real numbers and its banner; a shop showcase (cues, tables, frames, avatars, stickers, gifts, the season pass); a playing tip; a poll; a short scene or challenge |
| any | `dated` | tied to a weekday or a date and takes that day's slot: Monday morning "a new week" (ranking, Showdown and the Table of the week reset on Monday 00:00 UTC = 05:00 Tashkent), Sunday evening "the week closes", some Fridays, the start and the last day of every Season Pass month, 8 Dec, New Year, 14 Jan, 14 Feb, 8 and 21 March |
| evening | data | news made from real data (the weekly top three, player-count milestones, see below), when there is any |

Every post carries an inline **"▶ O'ynash · Play"** button and the same link in the text. The link is tracked per kind: `https://t.me/Billiard8PoolBot/Play?startapp=c_ch_<kind>` (`feat`, `quiz`, `city`, `shop`, `tip`, `poll`, `story`, `dated`, `data`), so `/src` (see "Ad-link counter") shows which kind brings players. A poll may refuse a button; the server then sends it without one and remembers that.

**The bank.** `server/channel_bank.json` (about 300 posts) plus the older `server/channel_posts.json` (59 posts, ids `L00...`, edited so that none says "one post a day"): feat 92, quiz 131, city 30, shop 24, tip 25, poll 18, story 14, dated 29. Nothing repeats until a whole list has gone out (the last 3 posts and the last 2 topics of a kind are also kept apart); a post of a particular date always beats a weekday post. Every number in the texts is read from the game's own data (cities, prizes, caps of the limited editions, ranking prizes, daily-cup hours, gifts, stickers, medals...); nothing is invented: no testimonials, no player counts, no promise that sounds like real money (a lint rejects such words), only virtual coins / 💵 / 💎. English comes next to every Uzbek text. The raw game screenshots are in `server/channel/` (`p..` first set, `t_<city>` the 30 tables, `c_..` cues, `s_..` shop, `m_..` match, `l_..` lobby, `x_..` pass / road / missions / friends / Showdown / profile); the posts use their **banners** (below).

**Banners.** Every text post goes out with a 1280x720 banner `server/channel/b_*.jpg` (139 of them) instead of a plain screenshot: a big gold headline in the game's own font, the game's own art (trophies, chests, coins, gifts, avatars, frames, cues, rings, city landmarks, its logo picture) and a small framed screenshot where that explains the feature, a green "BEPUL O'YNANG" button and the bot's name. They are drawn inside the game's own page (so the art and the font are the real ones) by `node tools/build_channel_banners.js` from `tools/channel_banners/specs.js` (one entry per topic; the 30 city banners come from `cities.json`; every post without a picture of its own gets one made from its title and topic, with a different picture for each tip). The script redraws only the banners whose entry changed (`server/channel/banners.json` keeps the hashes); `--force` draws all, `--only key,key` some, `--sheet out.png` writes contact sheets to look at. At start the server maps a post's picture to its banner (`b_<name>.jpg`, or `b_a_<post id>.jpg` for a post that had none); a post whose banner is missing simply uses the plain picture. Run `python3 tools/build_channel_bank.py` first when posts were added (the banners of the new posts are made from the bank file), then the banner script.

**Look of a post.** The headline is bold, the Uzbek body is a Telegram quote block, the English line is a collapsed quote block (tap to read), then the link and hashtags. If Telegram refuses the quote blocks the server posts that one in the old plain look.

**Editing the bank.** The texts live in `tools/channel_bank/*.txt` (one block per post, format in the header of `tools/build_channel_bank.py`; `cities.json` holds the numbers of the 30 tables). After a change run `python3 tools/build_channel_bank.py` from the repo root: it rebuilds `server/channel_bank.json` (the options of every quiz are shuffled, with a fixed seed, so the right answer is spread evenly over the four places) and lints it with the same checks the server uses (Telegram limits: quiz question 300, option 100, explanation 200, photo caption 1024; a missing picture; a bad id). If a post fails, the server prints `channel bank: N problems` at start and skips only those posts.

**Owner commands** (only `ADMIN_TG_ID` gets an answer): `/chan` shows today's posts (and the plan), the total, how much of each list has gone out in the current round and the next three days (times and kinds); `/chan now` posts the next one at once, `/chan now quiz` (or `feat`, `city`, `shop`, `tip`, `poll`, `story`) one of that kind. If the bot is not an administrator yet it says so once every 6 hours.

**Settings** (all optional): `CHAN_SLOTS="10:00,14:00,20:30"` or `CHAN_PER_DAY=1..6`, `CHAN_JITTER_MIN` (15), `CHAN_GAP_MIN` (45), `CHAN_END` (22:45), `CHAN_MAX_DAY` (slots + 2), `CHAN_SILENT` (1; `0` = every post makes a sound), `CHAN_NAMES=0` (no player names, see below). Test knobs: `CHAN_TICK_MS` (5 minutes), `CHAN_FIRST_MS`, `CHAN_TIME_SHIFT_MS` (moves the clock).

**Tests.** `node server/test_chan_plan.js` (79 checks, including: no post mentions 💵 (cash is never advertised in the channel), every text post has a banner and a caption within Telegram's limit) runs the planner and the real bank through 100 simulated days with a fake clock (exactly 3 posts a day, no repeats, 45-minute gaps, catch-up, the weekly rhythm, every dated post on its date, size limits, wording). `node server/test_chan_flow.js` starts the real server against a mock Telegram API (`TG_API_BASE`) with the slots set a few minutes into the past and checks that three posts go out (photo, quiz poll, evening kind), only the first one with sound, the button and tracked link, `/chan`, `/chan now`, and that a restart does not post the day again (add `nopollkb` to test polls that refuse the button, `noquote` to test that the posts go out plain when Telegram refuses quote blocks). Real Telegram itself could not be reached from the development sandbox, so the first live post is the real test: send `/chan now` and look at the channel.

### Posts made from real game data

With the same switch the server also posts from what really happens in the game. These take the **evening slot** (instead of the regular evening post) while any are waiting; a post older than 3 days is dropped:

- **Weekly results.** When a week closes (Monday 00:00 UTC) the top three of the world ranking are announced, with how many players competed. Fewer than three ranked players: nothing is posted. No prize amounts are ever mentioned.
- **Player-count milestones.** When the number of players who have played at least one game passes 50, 100, 250, 500, 1000, 2500, 5000, 10000, 25000, 50000 or 100000. The first run only records the current count, so nothing old is announced.

**Names need consent.** A player's name (and level) appears only if they switched on *Settings > Show my name in the channel* (`prefs.chanName`, off by default, stored on the server; a client that does not send it counts as "no"). ## Lobby voice room (talk before the match, up to 8 players)

- The lobby screen has a **🎤 Voice room** button under *Share / Challenge a friend*. Tap it: the browser asks for the microphone, then everybody in the lobby who has also joined the room hears each other (like the voice room of Mobile Legends). Buttons: the big one mutes / un-mutes, 🔊 silences the others for me, ✕ leaves the room. Each player slot shows 🎤 (in the room) / 🔇 (muted) / … (connecting) and a green ring around whoever is speaking; the line under the buttons says how many people are in the room, so a late joiner sees at once that friends are already talking. The menu's lobby chip shows 🎤 while the room is on.
- No microphone (blocked, missing, Telegram's iOS browser): the room still works as **listen-only**; tapping the button again retries the microphone. Same explanations as the in-match voice (`Voice.micFail`).
- How it works: a mesh of WebRTC calls (≤ 8 players, Opus, a few kbit/s each) - `LobbyVoice` in `index.html`. The server only relays the signalling: WS message `lvoice` (`hello` / `bye` / `offer` / `answer` / `ice` / `mute`, optional `to` = one member, otherwise everybody else in the lobby), accepted only from a member of that lobby and rate limited (`bucket(ws, "lvoice", 240, 100)`). The audio goes phone to phone, ICE servers (STUN / TURN) come from `/api/ice` like the in-match voice, so **mobile networks need the `TURN_*` variables** on Render.
- The player with the smaller id makes the offer; `hello` with `r` = "I started from scratch" resets old calls, `q` = "who is here?" (answered with the real hello). Calls are checked every second and rebuilt up to 5 times; hello is repeated every 8 s while somebody is not connected.
- While a match is on screen the in-match 🎤 uses the same, still open, microphone: the room says `bye` and comes back (hello again) the moment the player returns to the lobby / menu - without waiting for anything (see "One microphone" below). Leaving the lobby ends the room. Portals that mute the game (`__B8_MUTE`) mute the friends' voices too.
- Tested with 3 real Chromium instances against the real server (fake microphone device): all three connected pairwise, audio packets flowing both ways, mute / speaker / leave / late join / listen-only / suspend-resume (`lv_test.js`, 45 checks).
- **The microphone picture.** The 🎤 emoji is replaced everywhere (toasts, pop-ups, texts in every language, slot badges, the lobby chip) by one flat picture: a light microphone (capsule, U-shaped holder, stand) in a round dark-teal badge - compact and the same on every phone. `micShapes` / `micUri` / `MIC_SPAN` in `index.html`; a small `MutationObserver` turns any text node that contains 🎤 into text + `<span class="mico">`. The buttons use the same glyph (`Voice.IC.mic`). Muted = the same picture with a red slash (`.mico.off`), connecting = faded (`.mico.wait`).
- **Reward cards on the match result screen.** The little cards that pop up after a match ("-1 cup", "Showdown +20 points · +1 token", new medal...) used to sit in the middle and cover the player's picture and the table name. On the result screen they now go to the free top-right corner, smaller, and come one after another (one at a time) instead of stacking. CSS `body:has(#res.on) #toasts` and the cap in `pumpToast()`.
- **Reward pictures are shown whole.** The pictures that replace the glyphs in toasts and texts (cup 🏆, knight ♞, wheel, chest, friends...) were cropped by the toast's round-avatar rule (`object-fit:cover; border-radius:50%`): the knight had no head, the cup no crown. `.toast .ti img.ei` and a global `img.ei{object-fit:contain}` fix it.
- Audit of cropped / squashed pictures: a Playwright scan opened every screen and tab (plus reward pop-ups and every toast icon) and flagged each `<img>` / small `background-size: cover` box whose CSS crops or stretches it. Besides the toast pictures, only the Road-to-Cups city icons were squashed 12% (now `object-fit: contain`); what is left is intentional (city photos on the table cards, full-screen backdrops, round avatars, the home screen art that is scaled to the stage on purpose).

## The lobby as one room: voice at the table, watching, chat, joining a running tournament

The report from a friends' lobby (one friend inside Telegram): "the microphone does not turn on even in a normal browser", "in the next match his microphone does not work and he is frozen in the match", "the next player must not just wait - he must be able to come in and watch our lobby match, but nothing shows up for him", "all 8 must be able to talk", "add writing messages too".

- **Voice goes on at the table.** A match *of the lobby* (played or watched) no longer pauses the lobby's voice room: `LobbyVoice.matchOn()` is false for it, so all members - the two at the table, the ones waiting, the watchers, up to 8 - stay connected to each other the whole time (before, the room said `bye` and only a 1-to-1 call between the two opponents was left; whoever waited was cut off). The in-match 🎤 / 🔊 buttons of a lobby match are the room's microphone / speaker (`Voice.lobbyRoom()`; lit = my microphone is open in the room, no separate 1-to-1 call is made). Ordinary tables (random opponent, challenge) keep the 1-to-1 call.
- **A refused microphone is explained in a window.** The player who tapped 🎤 gets a window (not a 3-second toast) with the reason and the steps: permission denied / no microphone / busy / "open the game in its own window"; in Telegram's built-in browser: *"Telegram did not allow the microphone - tap Allow when Telegram asks, check Apps -> Telegram -> Permissions -> Microphone, then reopen the game; otherwise open the link in a normal browser; listening works"*. He still hears the others as a listener (`Voice.micFail(e, lobby, tap)`, `inAppBox`).
- **Watching from the lobby.** The live match of the bracket is a row with a clear **👁 Watch** button (`.lbw`, a blue pill) and a hint line; a stray global CSS rule `.live{position:absolute;...}` (the red "live" badge) used to throw that row to the top-left corner under the top bar, so the waiting player saw nothing - the row class is now `lblive`. A member who is not in the bracket sees "Tournament in progress" instead of "Waiting for the next round". Watching keeps the lobby voice room and has the lobby chat button.
- **Joining a running tournament.** The server no longer answers `lobbybusy` while a tournament runs: a free seat is enough. The newcomer sits in the lobby, gets the bracket (`tourstate` now goes to every member and carries `prizes`, `pool`, `fee`, `ids`), watches, talks and writes, and plays in the next tournament. The Open lobbies list shows a started lobby with a blue **👁 Watch** button instead of a grey "Started".
- **No frozen opponent at the next match.** A winner who stayed on the result screen (did not tap *Continue*) left his next opponent alone at the table. ## Adapting to the phone (Vivo Y31 report)

- **Root cause of the "different on another phone" layouts:** a phone held upright shows the page turned 90° (`html.rot`), and there the browser's own `vh` / `vw` units are the wrong axes (a 392x733 phone sized the result screen for a page 733 px tall that is really 392 px tall) - so the layout depended on the phone's shape: overlapping titles / chips / buttons on the result screen. - **Small pages are scaled, not cut:** the menus zoom down to at most 80% when the page is smaller than ~700x340 (`uiz`), instead of overlapping or dropping pieces.
- **Menus are 12% smaller on phones / tablets** (main pointer coarse): `uiz` = 0.74 x the size-based value (never below 0.62), so more of every list fits on the page (the owner: "the menus are too big"). A computer keeps its size.

## HD pack (sharp avatars, frames, cues, badges, icons, ball textures)
The pictures are drawn for a 1340×720 screen; a 2400×1080 phone or a 4K tablet shows them 2–3 times bigger, so small ones looked soft. The fix is a second download, like the "HD resource pack" of big mobile games:
- `tools/build_hd.py` makes a sharper copy of the small pictures (**1541 of them, 50.6 MB**: avatars 100 → 400 px, frames ×2, cues 1160 → 2320 px, badges / medals / icons ×3, ball textures 320 → 640 px) with Real-ESRGAN running on the CPU
  (`tools/hd/esrgan.py`, onnxruntime, no torch, no GPU, no credits; `pip install onnx onnxruntime pillow numpy`; about 1 hour for everything, resumable: existing files are skipped). The copies are `web/hd/<hash>.webp` (the hash is the one of `img/<hash>.<ext>`), kept in git.
  Big pictures (halls, backgrounds, tables, > 1300 px) are left alone, they are already 2–3K.
- **Not every picture may be bigger**: some are measured in pixels by the code or the CSS (table rails and pocket rings with a pixel table next to them, 9-slice `border-image` borders, `background-size:auto`, pictures drawn with `drawImage(img, x, y)` or shown with no CSS size). `tools/build_hd.py` skips them (`EXCLUDE_OWNERS`, the CSS scan `css_risky()`, and `tools/hd/exclude.txt`, 527 pictures).
  After a big UI change run `node tools/hd_risk_audit.js out.json` (`CRAWL=1` also clicks every button of every screen), add what it finds to `tools/hd/exclude.txt` and run `python3 tools/build_hd.py --prune`.
- `tools/build_web.py` lists the copies in `web/assets.json` (`hd`: `[base name, size of the HD copy]`) and keeps every picture that has one as a file (never inline), so it can be swapped. Rebuild the light build after `build_hd.py`.
- The start-up script at the top of `index.html` downloads the standard pack first, then the HD pack (its own progress: "Downloading HD graphics (x MB / y MB)") into the cache `billiard8-hd`, and reloads the page once. `sw.js` answers a request for `img/<hash>.<ext>` with the copy kept in `billiard8-hd` when there is one,

- Settings → Graphics has a new level "4K" (the screen's own pixels). The default level is 2K on strong devices (6 GB+, 8+ cores) and Full HD on the others; hand-drawn avatars are painted at 256 px on sharp screens; balls are painted up to 96 / 72 px, from mip-mapped textures (the HD ones on big screens).

## Lobby matches that lose the connection (freeze / "left the lobby by itself")

The owner's report: in a lobby match the game "left the lobby by itself", froze, and the other player stood frozen in the lobby while the first one kept shooting. Four causes, all fixed:

- **One handler for "match".** `Net.on` keeps a single handler per message type; the quick-match search (`findOpponent`) used to install its own `match` handler and keep it, so every later bracket match of the lobby was answered with `resign` (the player "left by itself"). There is now one router (right after `Net.on("oppBack")`): a match with `tour` goes to the lobby (`lbMatch`; a lobby this phone has already left → `resign` at once), any other goes to the search that is waiting (`searchWait`), a match nobody waits for is given up. The search also leaves the lobby on the client (`lbDrop`: the server takes the player out of the lobby when he queues), and playing a table in the middle of a running tournament is refused.
- **A bracket match that waited behind a result screen** (`LB.queued`) started only from that screen's "Continue". A 1 s timer now starts it as soon as the player is on a menu screen with no result / VS screen / pop-up in front. `lbMatch` ignores the same match twice (the server sends it again when a phone reconnects).
- **Dead connections.** A phone that changed network / came back from the background keeps a socket that looks open but is dead. The client pings (`{t:"ping"}` → `{t:"pong"}`) every ~4.5 s, after `visibilitychange` / `online` it must be answered within 5 s, and a connection silent for 15 s is dropped on purpose (`dead()` in the `Net` closure) → `Net.on("close")` → `netLost()` (in a match) or `lbResume()` (in a lobby, now also in a running tournament).
- **A drop no longer ends the match.** The server keeps the match `MATCH_GRACE` (30 s, env `MATCH_GRACE_MS`) for a phone whose socket closed: the opponent sees "Raqib ulanishini yo'qotdi — kutilmoqda…", then "Raqib qaytdi ✓"; only after the grace time he wins (`end`, reason `left`). The lobby seat is kept too (also in a running tournament; `sweepLobby` gives it up after `LOBBY_GRACE`).
  - **resume:** the phone reconnects (every 1.2 s, up to 34 s), sends `{t:"resume", room, got}`; the server puts the new socket into the old one's seat (`swapSocket` / `adoptRoom`: lobby, bracket, live match), answers `{t:"resumed", rx}` and replays what the opponent played that it missed. A finished match answers with its `end` (kept 10 min in `recentEnd`), an unknown one with `noresume` (server restarted).
  - **No shot is lost, none is played twice:** the server numbers the relayed `shot` / `state` messages (`n`, kept in `room.log` per seat) and counts what it received from each seat (`room.rx`: shot / state / result / resign); the phone drops messages with an `n` it has seen (`rxN`) and keeps what it sent (`sent`), so after `resumed.rx` it sends again only the messages the server did not get. A lobby `rejoin` in a running tournament also re-sends `match` + the opponent's log.
- Test: `node tools/test_lobby_drop.js` (2 real browsers: search then lobby match, queued match starts by itself, a dead connection, a closed connection, a phone that never returns; `MATCH_GRACE_MS=6000`).

## Online match: the opponent sees my cue, and both phones throw the same dice

## Voice: one microphone, a remembered choice, listening without a microphone

The report: "after ~15 games the lobby voice stopped", "the microphone asks for permission again and again", "if listening is on I must hear the opponent, with listening off nothing is heard".

- **One microphone (`MicHub` in `index.html`).** The lobby room and the in-match voice used to ask the browser for a new microphone at every match (`getUserMedia` x2 per match) and to wait for the answer before the room came back - a phone that asks again (or never answers) left the room dead. Now there is ONE stream for the whole app: it is asked for once, muted with `track.enabled` (never stopped), and handed to whoever needs it; the room comes back at once after a match. It is let go only 90 s after the player has left every lobby and online match (and when he taps ✕ / hangs up), then taken again silently the next time voice starts.
- **The choice is remembered** (`localStorage b8_voice` = `{mic, mute, spkOff}`): tap 🎤 once and every later lobby / online match starts with the voice on by itself (no tap, no question); mute and speaker-off are kept as well. Switching off = ✕ in the lobby, or hanging up (tap while the call is not connected, right-click) in a match - only then the saved "microphone on" is forgotten. A match that merely ends keeps it.
- **Listening needs no microphone.** In a match: when the opponent starts talking and my speaker is on, I join as a listener at once (receive-only call, no question asked); with the speaker off nothing joins and the sound is muted; switching the speaker on while he talks joins. The same in the lobby: when somebody in the room has an open microphone and my speaker is on, I join the room as a listener. The speaker is one switch (lobby and match share it, it is remembered).
- **A dead call is rebuilt.** The lobby room starts a call again from scratch (`heal`) when it is not connected for 30 s although both want it, when the phone comes back from another app (`visibilitychange`), and the retry counters start over every 30 s; a match offer that is never answered is repeated after 10 s; the in-match signalling is handled one message after another (no two offers at once).
